Hi! Welcome to my website. Currently, I am a postdoctoral fellow at FGV-CIPE, the Center for Studies on Education Policy Implementation at FGV EAESP, a fellowship run in partnership with the Stanford Graduate School of Education. I hold a PhD in Public Administration and Government from FGV EAESP (2022), an MA and a BA in Economics from FGV EESP (2016), and a BA in Social Sciences from the University of São Paulo (2013).

From 2021 to 2026, I was a researcher at the Instituto Mobilidade e Desenvolvimento Social (Imds), where I worked on Plataforma Impacto, a public platform of evaluated policies organized by life stage, and on monitoring and evaluation projects in several policy areas, including education, social assistance and labor market policy.

Before Imds, I was a researcher at FGV CLEAR, the Center for Learning on Evaluation and Results for Brazil and Lusophone Africa at FGV EESP (2015–2017), and a data analyst at MGov Brasil, now Movva (2017). I was a short-term consultant for the World Bank's Poverty and Equity Global Practice, in Washington and São Paulo.

  1. Artificial Intelligence, Writing Achievement and Pedagogy: Evidence from High Schools in Brazil

    withcom Bruno Ferman ande Lycia Lima

    This version: September 2025.Esta versão: setembro de 2025. R&R, Journal of Political Economy: Microeconomics

    Materials:Materiais: J-PAL Evidence to Policy|J-PAL Summary|AEA RCT Registry|Pre-analysis planPlano de pré-análise

    Media coverage:Na imprensa: Nexo|Valor Econômico|O Globo|Porvir

    Ages 15–2915–29 anosTechnology for learningEstrutura física e tecnológica para as aprendizagensAcademic achievementDesempenho acadêmico e técnico

    Can a machine grade the essays so that teachers can teach the writing? In a statewide experiment in Espírito Santo, AI feedback raised scores on the essay of Brazil's high-stakes college exam, and adding human graders bought nicer comments but no extra points. The policy choice is between buying more grading and buying more teacher time, and the data point to time.Uma máquina pode corrigir as redações para que o professor ensine a escrever? Num experimento em toda a rede do Espírito Santo, a correção por inteligência artificial elevou a nota da redação do Enem, e somar corretores humanos melhorou os comentários, mas não a nota. A escolha de política é entre comprar mais correção e comprar mais tempo do professor, e os dados apontam para o tempo.

    This paper studies educational technologies that use artificial and human intelligence to enhance writing instruction. In a large-scale experiment, providing students with an online platform to practice and enabling teachers to outsource grading and feedback tasks to artificial intelligence improved essay scores on a high-stakes exam. Adding human graders to circumvent potential limitations of this automated system improved the perceived quality of feedback, but did not affect the extent to which this enhanced educational technology improved achievement. Both technologies shifted teachers' tasks to individualized instruction, suggesting that teachers used both sets of inputs as complements to the educational inputs they provide to develop complex writing skills.

  2. Better Early Childhoods of the Poor and Cohort Dynamics in Learning

    withcom Carolina Melo

    Version 2, November 2026.Versão 2, novembro de 2026.

    Materials:Materiais: SlidesSlides

    Ages 0–30–3 anosParenting supportApoio à parentalidadeAcademic achievementDesempenho acadêmico e técnico

    Does visiting poor families when their babies are young show up in the classroom ten years later? Rio Grande do Sul's home-visiting program, Primeira Infância Melhor, raised test scores at ages 10–11 by about 0.08 SD, most for the weakest students and for children reached in utero or as infants. Start before the cradle, not at the school gate.Visitar famílias pobres quando os filhos são bebês aparece na sala de aula dez anos depois? O Primeira Infância Melhor, do Rio Grande do Sul, elevou as notas aos 10–11 anos em cerca de 0,08 desvio-padrão, sobretudo entre os alunos mais fracos e as crianças alcançadas ainda na gestação ou no primeiro ano de vida. A política começa antes do berço, não no portão da escola.

    This paper examines how broad support for poor families during their children's early childhood affects learning of foundational skills in middle childhood. Using the rollout of a large-scale public policy called Primeira Infância Melhor (PIM, or Better Early Childhood) and a cohort difference-in-differences approach that leverages variation in age at first exposure to the program, we show that: (i) PIM's arrival in a municipality raised Language and Mathematics standardized test scores at ages 10 to 11, at around 0.08σ for exposed cohorts; (ii) effects are large for very low performers, who shift literacy and numeracy thresholds that children are supposed to attain during middle childhood; (iii) reaching children in utero or in the first year of life generates larger effects than reaching them later.

  3. Owl Mothers: Care around Birth, the Counting of Deaths and Learning in Pernambuco

    Draft, November 2026.Rascunho, novembro de 2026.

    Materials:Materiais: SlidesSlides

    Ages 0–30–3 anosMaternal and child healthAtenção à saúde materno-infantilAcademic achievementDesempenho acadêmico e técnico

    What did Pernambuco's Mãe Coruja, which follows poor women from pregnancy and pays a baby kit for seven prenatal visits, change? Children exposed before age 6 score about 0.1 SD higher at ages 10–11 and repeat less, births moved into hospitals, and infant mortality cannot be judged, because deaths started being counted exactly where the program arrived.O que mudou o Mãe Coruja Pernambucana, que acompanha mulheres pobres desde a gestação e dá um enxoval a quem faz sete consultas de pré-natal? Crianças expostas antes dos 6 anos têm notas cerca de 0,1 desvio-padrão maiores aos 10–11 anos e repetem menos, os partos foram para o hospital, e a mortalidade infantil não pode ser avaliada, porque os óbitos passaram a ser contados justamente onde o programa chegou.

    This paper studies Mãe Coruja Pernambucana, the "owl mother" program that since 2008 has enrolled pregnant women in Pernambuco's poorest municipalities, followed them through prenatal care and delivery and their children up to age five, and given a baby kit to women who attend seven or more prenatal visits. The program reached 103 of the state's 185 municipalities in 2008–2010, selected by a rule on infant mortality. Using the design of our evaluation of Rio Grande do Sul's Primeira Infância Melhor, children exposed for one to three years score about 0.1 standard deviations higher at ages 10 to 11 and are 4.6 points less likely to have repeated a grade, but the gains do not grow with exposure. Births in hospital, four or more prenatal visits and cesarean sections rise under every comparison; seven or more visits rise by 12 points against all non-adopters but by only 2 against matched ones; health at birth barely moves. Infant mortality cannot be evaluated with these data, because the registration of deaths improved fastest exactly where the program arrived.

  4. The Equity Case for Phonics-Based Literacy Instruction

    Materials:Materiais: SlidesSlides

    Ages 6–106–10 anosPedagogy and school managementProcessos pedagógicos e de gestão em redes escolaresAcademic achievementDesempenho acadêmico e técnico

    Revisits three randomized evaluations of phonics-based programs (South Africa, Uganda, Colombia) and Mississippi’s reform: gains reach most of the baseline distribution, boys catch up with girls, gaps by SES are unchanged.Revisita três avaliações aleatorizadas de programas baseados em fônica (África do Sul, Uganda, Colômbia) e a reforma do Mississippi: os ganhos alcançam a maior parte da distribuição inicial, os meninos alcançam as meninas e as diferenças por nível socioeconômico não mudam.

    Children start school with unequal phonological awareness and oral language, and these skills predict who learns to read. We ask whether explicit, systematic phonics instruction narrows or widens the resulting gaps. We revisit the public data of three cluster-randomized evaluations of phonics-based programs in South Africa, Uganda and Colombia (7,400 children in 289 schools) and state-level data on Mississippi’s 2013 literacy reform. Following Muralidharan and Sundararaman (2011), we estimate effects at each percentile of baseline skills, including phonological awareness. When teachers were coached, effects were positive across most of the baseline distribution but not larger for children who started behind. Boys gained 0.17–0.18 standard deviations more than girls, narrowing the gender gap. Effects did not differ significantly by socioeconomic status. In Mississippi, every racial and income group gained, Black students most. Phonics-based instruction raises the floor; narrowing gaps requires delivery and remediation.

  5. The Payoff to Early Literacy: Evidence from the Near-Eradication of Non-Readers in Ceará, Brazil

    Version 1, November 2026.Versão 1, novembro de 2026.

    Materials:Materiais: SlidesSlides

    Ages 6–106–10 anosPedagogy and school managementProcessos pedagógicos e de gestão em redes escolaresAcademic achievementDesempenho acadêmico e técnico

    What is it worth to read by age seven? Ceará cut the share of second graders who could not read from half to 6% in five years. Each 10 points more fluent readers raised fifth-grade scores by about 0.06 SD, and wiping out non-readers emptied the bottom of the fifth-grade distribution. By ninth grade the gain is gone, and not because weaker students stayed in school. Early literacy is a foundation, not an inoculation.Quanto vale ler aos sete anos? Em cinco anos, o Ceará reduziu de metade para 6% os alunos do 2º ano que não sabiam ler. Cada 10 pontos a mais de leitores fluentes elevou as notas do 5º ano em cerca de 0,06 desvio-padrão, e eliminar os não leitores esvaziou a base da distribuição no 5º ano. No 9º ano o ganho desaparece, e não porque alunos mais fracos permaneceram na escola. Alfabetizar na idade certa é alicerce, não vacina.

    Between 2007 and 2012 the Brazilian state of Ceará cut the share of second graders who could not read from one half to 6%. We link each municipality's measured second-grade literacy to the same cohort's fifth- and ninth-grade national test scores. Using untreated ninth-grade cohorts in the same municipality and year as a comparison group, we find that 10 percentage points more fully literate second graders raise fifth-grade Portuguese by 0.06 standard deviations and mathematics by 0.07. Eliminating non-readers shrinks the low-achieving tail but leaves mean scores unchanged. The gains fade by ninth grade, and selective dropout does not explain the fade-out.

  6. What Does Universal Full-Day High School Buy? Learning, Youth Homicides and Teen Births in Piauí, Brazil

    withcom Samuel Franco ande Bruna Goussain

    Materials:Materiais: Online appendixApêndice online|Slides

    Ages 15–2915–29 anosPedagogy and school managementProcessos pedagógicos e de gestão em redes escolaresRisk behaviorComportamento de risco

    What does a state buy when it keeps every high school student in school for eight hours a day? In Piauí, learning gains grew to 0.10 SD after three years, half of what the earlier selective schools suggested, with no fall in youth homicides or teen births. Gains are similar across sex, race and family income, but converted schools serve fewer older and night students. Each real spent returns 12 to 55 centavos of lifetime earnings, about what youth job training delivers.O que um estado compra ao manter todos os alunos do ensino médio na escola oito horas por dia? No Piauí, o ganho de aprendizagem chegou a 0,10 desvio-padrão em três anos, metade do que as escolas seletivas da fase anterior sugeriam, sem queda nos homicídios de jovens nem na gravidez na adolescência. Os ganhos são parecidos por sexo, cor e renda, mas as escolas convertidas passam a atender menos alunos mais velhos e do noturno. Cada real gasto rende de 12 a 55 centavos de renda ao longo da vida, perto do que entregam os programas de qualificação de jovens.

    Between 2023 and 2025, the Brazilian state of Piauí moved every state high school to an eight-hour school day. We use the staggered timing of school conversions to estimate its effects. Learning gains grew with exposure, from 0.02 standard deviations after one year to 0.10 after three, when the first converted cohort finished high school. Dropout and pass rates did not change, and converted schools neither pushed students out nor attracted better-prepared ones. Gains were larger in schools serving more advantaged students. They are also half the size of those from the state's earlier, selective phase, which suggests that selective rollouts overstate what universal ones deliver. Youth homicides did not fall, and teen births did not change. Each real of net public spending buys 12 to 55 centavos of after-tax lifetime earnings, close to youth job training and below most college aid programs.

  7. Delivering Health at School and Educational Outcomes: Evidence from Public Schools in Brazil

    withcom Lia Braga ande Rudi Rocha

    Materials:Materiais: SlidesSlides

    Ages 6–106–10 anosHealth in communities and schoolsSaúde nas comunidades e na escolaSchool flow and retentionFluxo, frequência e retenção escolar

    What happens when the Family Health team walks into the primary school? When Brazilian municipalities joined the Health at School Program, dropout in grades 1–5 fell by 0.9 percentage point, two-fifths of the counterfactual, more so in vulnerable places, while test scores did not move. For policy, health at school works as a way to keep children in school, not to make them learn more.O que acontece quando a equipe de Saúde da Família entra na escola? Nos municípios que aderiram ao Programa Saúde na Escola, o abandono do 1º ao 5º ano caiu 0,9 ponto percentual, dois quintos do contrafactual, mais nos lugares mais vulneráveis, e a proficiência não mudou. Para a política pública, saúde na escola funciona como instrumento de permanência, não de aprendizagem.

    This paper investigates the educational impacts of a policy-driven change in health services, ranging from anthropometric measurement, ophthalmological screening and referrals to specialized care to coordinated efforts to identify and fight endemic diseases, available to public school students in Brazil. Using imputation event studies on a panel of all Brazilian municipalities from 2007 to 2017, we find that adoption raised the share of public-school students covered by the program by 71 percentage points and reduced dropout in grades 1–5 by 0.89 percentage point (40% of the counterfactual mean) and evasion in grades 6–9 by 0.46 point. We find no gain in test scores and no effect in high school, which the program did not target. Effects are larger in more vulnerable municipalities and schools, and the gradient lines up with the control of endemic diseases rather than with screening services.

  8. Early-Life Primary Care and Learning in School: Evidence from the Expansion of Brazil's Family Health Program

    withcom Letícia Nunes ande Pedro H. Chaves Maia

    Materials:Materiais: SlidesSlides

    Does a health worker at the door in early childhood make a better student in fifth grade? Following how Brazil's Family Health Program spread across municipalities from the mid-1990s, we find that early access to primary care raised learning in fifth grade. Universal health coverage is also an education policy.Um agente de saúde batendo à porta na primeira infância forma um aluno melhor no 5º ano?     We study the effects of early access to primary health care on a rich set of measures of learning and schooling in middle childhood and adolescence, leveraging the expansion of Brazil's national community-based health program from the mid-1990s to the mid-2000s. Overall, the variation we explore to identify causal effects is directly relevant to assessing the benefits of expanding universal health coverage for aggregate human capital accumulation in educational systems.

  9. From Apprentice to Formal Worker: Teenagers' Long-Run Careers under Brazil's Apprenticeship Mandate

    withcom Raphael Corbi ande Matheus Leal

    Materials:Materiais: Imds Technical NoteNota Técnica Imds

    Media coverage:Na imprensa: Valor Econômico

    Ages 15–2915–29 anosActive labor market policiesPolíticas ativas de trabalhoLabor marketMercado de trabalho

    Is a teenage apprenticeship a door into formal work, or just cheap labor? After Brazil's 2000 apprenticeship mandate turned apprenticeships into the main first formal job at ages 15–17, an extra apprenticeship raised formal earnings at ages 25–29 by about a third of the pre-reform mean. The mandate looks less like a subsidy to firms and more like a stepping stone for workers.Ser jovem aprendiz abre a porta do emprego formal ou é só mão de obra barata? Depois que a Lei da Aprendizagem de 2000 fez da aprendizagem o principal primeiro emprego formal aos 15–17 anos, cada contrato a mais elevou a renda formal aos 25–29 anos em cerca de um terço da média anterior à reforma. A cota parece menos um subsídio às empresas e mais um degrau para os trabalhadores.

    In December 2000, Brazil turned a dormant apprenticeship system into a mass entry route into formal employment for teenagers: within five years, apprenticeships went from 13.9% to 80.5% of first formal jobs under fixed-term contracts at ages 15–17. We follow the affected cohorts in linked employer–employee records until age 30. Comparing cohorts before and after the reform in microregions where take-up expanded a lot and a little, we find that an additional apprenticeship increased the share of time spent in formal employment at ages 25–29 by 0.131, almost entirely in open-ended jobs, and monthly formal earnings at those ages by 330.2 BRL, about a third of the pre-reform mean. The employment effect survives state-by-cohort fixed effects but not the most conservative heterogeneity-robust estimator, which instead finds a precisely estimated gain in earnings; effects on accumulated formal experience are positive but imprecise. We find no robust effects on hourly wages or schooling, and the gains concentrate among entrants with more than primary schooling. A second design, based on the age-18 eligibility limit, reaches similar conclusions in the late twenties once we net out the pre-program difference between 17- and 18-year-old entrants.

    withcom Vitor Possebom

    Journal of Business & Economic Statistics, 2024.

    Materials:Materiais: Published versionVersão publicada|arXiv versionVersão no arXiv

    Ages 15–2915–29 anosActive labor market policiesPolíticas ativas de trabalhoLabor marketMercado de trabalho

    How many people found formal jobs because of a training program, and not in spite of it? With sample selection the honest answer is a range: among always-employed women in Colombia's Jóvenes en Acción, between 10.2% and 13.4% moved to formal work because of the program. Evaluations that report a single number may be claiming more than the data know.Quantas pessoas conseguiram um emprego formal por causa de um programa de qualificação, e não apesar dele? Com seleção amostral, a resposta honesta é um intervalo: entre as mulheres sempre empregadas do Jóvenes en Acción, na Colômbia, de 10,2% a 13,4% passaram ao emprego formal por causa do programa. Avaliações que reportam um único número podem estar afirmando mais do que os dados sabem.

    A paper on partial identification. When outcomes are observed only for a selected sample, the data cannot pin down the probability of causation (the share of people whose outcome was caused by a program), but they can bound it. We derive sharp bounds for individuals who are always observed, under three increasingly restrictive sets of assumptions. Among always-employed women in Colombia's Jóvenes en Acción, between 10.2% and 13.4% moved to the formal labor market because of the program, although the 90% confidence region does not rule out a lower bound of zero.Um artigo sobre identificação parcial. Quando os resultados só são observados para uma amostra selecionada, os dados não determinam a probabilidade de causalidade (a parcela de pessoas cujo resultado foi causado por um programa), mas permitem delimitá-la. Derivamos limites estreitos para os indivíduos sempre observados, sob três conjuntos de hipóteses cada vez mais restritivos. Entre as mulheres sempre empregadas do Jóvenes en Acción, na Colômbia, de 10,2% a 13,4% passaram ao mercado de trabalho formal por causa do programa, embora a região de confiança de 90% não descarte um limite inferior igual a zero.

  2. Brazil: Employment and Inequality Trends

    withcom Sergio Firpo, Alysson Portella ande Giovanna Úbida

    InEm Tasks, Skills, and Institutions: The Changing Nature of Work and Inequality, ed. C. Gradín, P. Lewandowski, S. Schotte and K. Sen, Oxford University Press, 2023, pp. 229–252.

    Materials:Materiais: Published versionVersão publicada|WIDER Working Paper 2021/162WIDER Working Paper 2021/162

    We use different sources of data on the task content of jobs to study how occupations, and the intensity of routine tasks embodied in them, explain changes in employment and earnings in Brazil. We find some evidence of polarization in earnings, but not in employment. Changes in the earnings structure explain most of the fall in inequality between 2003 and 2012, while changes in composition dominate its rise between 2012 and 2019; both are driven by education and its returns. The contribution of routine-task content to inequality is, in general, small.Usamos diferentes fontes de dados sobre o conteúdo de tarefas das ocupações para estudar como as ocupações, e a intensidade de tarefas rotineiras nelas embutida, explicam as mudanças no emprego e nos rendimentos no Brasil. A contribuição do conteúdo rotineiro das tarefas para a desigualdade é, em geral, pequena.

  3. Technical Education, Non-Cognitive Skills and Labor Market Outcomes: Experimental Evidence from Brazil

    withcom Juliana Camargo, Lycia Lima ande André Portela Souza

    IZA Journal of Labor Economics, 2021.

    Materials:Materiais: Published version (open access)Versão publicada (acesso aberto)

    Ages 15–2915–29 anosActive labor market policiesPolíticas ativas de trabalhoLabor marketMercado de trabalho

    Do free technical courses for public high school students pay off in the labor market? In a waiting-list experiment in Santa Catarina, women gained 21 points in employment, more than 50% in earnings and large gains in non-cognitive skills, while men gained nothing.     A waiting-list randomized trial in four municipalities of Santa Catarina evaluates scholarships for free technical and vocational courses offered to current and former public high school students.   1. Políticas Públicas e Desigualdades de Aprendizado por Nível Socioeconômico: O Quanto É Possível Nivelar o Campo de Jogo?

    withcom Samuel Franco

    Imds Technical NoteNota Técnica Imds, 2025.

    This technical note analyzes socioeconomic differences in Portuguese language learning among public school students in Brazil, combining SAEB microdata for the 5th year of elementary school with the socioeconomic index of their schools, from 2009 to 2023. In 2023, only 3 out of every 10 students in the bottom tenth of the index reached the adequate level, compared to 8 out of 10 in the top tenth. Between 2009 and 2023, all tenths improved by similar amounts, so socioeconomic differences in learning are similar to those of 2009.   2. Programa Jovem Aprendiz, Empregabilidade e Renda Formal do Trabalho: Uma Análise das Primeiras Gerações Expostas à Lei da Aprendizagem de 2000Programa Jovem Aprendiz, Empregabilidade e Renda Formal do Trabalho: Uma Análise das Primeiras Gerações Expostas à Lei da Aprendizagem de 2000

    withcom Raphael Corbi, Sergio Guimarães, Matheus Leal ande Paulo Tafner

    Imds Technical NoteNota Técnica Imds, 2025.

    Media coverage:Na imprensa: Valor Econômico

    The Apprenticeship Law (Law 10.097/2000), which created the Jovem Aprendiz Program, set up a specific hiring regime and minimum quotas for apprentices: from 2001 to 2005, the number of employed apprentices grew more than tenfold. Plataforma Impacto

Que programas já foram testados para melhorar a alfabetização, reduzir a evasão escolar ou aumentar a renda de jovens? A Plataforma Impacto, do Imds, reúne em acesso público e gratuito avaliações de impacto de políticas e programas de desenvolvimento humano e mobilidade social, no Brasil e no mundo. Cada ficha descreve, em linguagem direta, o desenho do programa, seus efeitos e os mecanismos que os explicam.

A busca é organizada por fase da vida, da gestação à vida adulta, e pode ser refinada por país, tipo de programa e resultado que se quer alcançar. Assim, a plataforma responde a perguntas práticas, como “qual o efeito deste programa?” e “como melhorar este indicador?”. Para gestores públicos, equipes técnicas, legisladores, jornalistas e organizações da sociedade civil, é um ponto de partida para desenhar ou rever uma política com base no que já foi avaliado.
